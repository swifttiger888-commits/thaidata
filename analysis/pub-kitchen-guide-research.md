## 0. What the guide will say (TL;DR)

- Three ways a Thai kitchen ends up in a pub: **in-house kitchen** (pub employs cooks), **kitchen residency/takeover** (independent business rents/rev-shares the kitchen), **taking on the whole pub** (tenancy/lease, tied or free-of-tie) `[2]`.
- The residency model is the common one: fixed rent or **15–25% of dine-in sales**, usually a 6-month minimum, deposit 4–6 weeks, kitchen equipment normally included `[2]`.
- The Thai kitchen is usually a **separate food business** — it registers with the council itself (at least 28 days before trading, free, cannot be refused) `[3][4][17]`; our FSA data shows all three registration variants live in the UK today.
- **Tax:** each business pays its own. Food revenue → the kitchen operator (Income Tax or Corporation Tax) `[20][24]`; rent/commissions received → the pub. VAT: on-premises catering and **hot takeaway are standard-rated** (20%), cold takeaway can be zero-rated `[6]`; separate registration is fine when the split is genuine — HMRC explicitly names pub bar/kitchen splits, and says franchised "shop within a shop" arrangements are normally not treated as artificial separation `[22]`.
- **Licensing:** selling alcohol stays on the pub's **premises licence** (with a designated premises supervisor) `[15]`; hot food between 11pm–5am needs **late-night refreshment** on the licence `[7][8][15]`.
- Employing staff: register as employer for PAYE before the first payday `[21]`; workplace pension duties start with the first eligible staff member (22+, ≥£10,000) `[23]`.
- Insurance is the kitchen operator's responsibility (public/products liability minimum; employer's liability if staff) `[2]`.
- **Why pubs want this:** wet-led pubs lack kitchens and kitchen staff, and food keeps customers in the building longer `[11]` — a Thai operator solves both.

## 1. The three routes (how it works)

### Route A — In-house (pub employs the kitchen)
- The pub runs the kitchen itself; any cooks are **employees of the pub**. Registration & food-safety duties sit with the pub `[3][17]`.
- Least common in the Thai pattern — it depends on the pub finding (and affording) chefs, which the trade press describes as the exact pain point `[11]`.

### Route B — Kitchen residency / takeover (the classic Thai-in-pub model)
- Independent food business takes over the pub kitchen: "a fully equipped commercial kitchen within a pub or bar venue, made available to an external food operator on a residency basis" `[2]`.
- Terms: min. term typically 6 months; deposit 4–6 weeks rent; **fixed rent (£700–£1,500/mo regional; £1,500–£4,000 London) or revenue share 15–25% of dine-in sales**, sometimes lower rent + smaller % `[2]`.
- Normally included: kitchen + equipment, access to the venue's licence, front-of-house dine-in space, often utilities. Not included: stock, staffing, hygiene certificates, insurance, marketing `[2]`.
- The kitchen operator runs its own business: own food registration `[3][17]`, own insurance `[2]`, own pricing.
- Support for the "rides on the pub's registration" nuance (Pattern 1 in our data): the FSA record can stay registered to the pub, but a **separate food business must register in its own right** `[3][17]` — the split matters for both hygiene inspections and tax.

### Route C — Take on the whole pub (tenancy or lease)
- Tied tenancy (pubco supplies the beer, cheaper rent) vs free-of-tie. Large pubcos: tied tenants have Pubs Code rights, incl. the **Market Rent Only option** at rent review/renewal — the right to compare and choose a free-of-tie tenancy at market rent `[18][19]`.
- Real example: **Nartnapa Thai Kitchen** — Bangkok-trained chef, took over a closed pub in Lynton, now trades also from The Queens pub; UK training path (Chef Yingsak), takeaway → restaurant → pub `[10]`.
- This route = running a licensed premises: premises licence, DPS, alcohol duties `[15]`.

## 2. Registration & compliance (what the cook must do)

- Register the food business with the local authority **at least 28 days before trading**; free; cannot be refused; must be done when starting **or taking over** an existing business `[3][17]`.
- Register **each premises separately**; the kitchen at a pub is a premises for its own operator `[3][17]`.
- Multiple trading names (e.g. delivery-platform name) go on **one** registration; extra names later = notify the council, no re-registration `[17]`.
- Must operate **HACCP / SFBB-style food safety management**; recommended (not compulsory) hygiene training; 14-allergen information duties `[17]`.
- Our own data (unique to ThaiData): the three registration patterns seen nationally — (1) kitchen under the pub's registration (pub-typed record, kitchen name absent), (2) kitchen named but recorded as the pub, (3) kitchen separately registered (own record + rating). 22 live pub-kitchen venues tracked at /pubs/.

## 3. Tax map — "who pays what"

| Item | Who | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Income/Corporation Tax on food profit | Kitchen operator `[20][24]` | Sole trader → Income Tax; Ltd → Corporation Tax `[24]` |
| Tax on rent/commission received | Pub | Normal trading income |
| VAT on food | Each business registers separately | On-premises + **hot takeaway standard-rated**; cold takeaway zero-rated (not crisps/sweets/certain drinks) `[6]` |
| VAT threshold | £90,000 rolling 12-month turnover | Register within 30 days of breaching; voluntary registration possible `[13]` |
| Payroll | Employer | Register as employer before first payday (PAYE ref) `[21]`; auto-enrol eligible staff (22+, ≥£10k) `[23]` |
| Business rates | On the non-domestic property | Bill from the council; VO for rateable value queries `[14]` |
| Insurance | Kitchen operator | Public & products liability minimum; employer's liability if staff `[2]` |

**The split-business question (asked constantly):** HMRC's own policy statement lists "pubs in which the bar and catering may be artificially separated" as a case it examines — but: "truly franchised 'shop within a shop' arrangements will not normally be considered artificial" `[22]`. Practitioner thread (2012) adds the practical test: written contract, market rent, separate books/bank; a peppercorn rent with no formal contract lost at appeal `[5]`. (Label `[5]` as practitioner commentary, not law.)

## 4. Licensing (England & Wales focus — Scotland differs)

- Alcohol: premises licence + designated premises supervisor (personal licence holder); staff can't sell alcohol unless authorised by a PLH `[15]`.
- **Late-night refreshment**: hot food/drink to the public 11pm–5am requires it on the premises licence `[7][15]`; check the pub's licence actually covers your closing time — schedule change = licence variation `[8]`.
- Deregulation Act 2015 lets councils exempt some LNR premises `[7]` — check locally.
- Fees vary by rateable-value band `[15]`.

## 5. Why pubs want a Thai kitchen (colour + context)

- Wet-led pubs often have no kitchen or only a back-bar; recruiting kitchen staff is the #1 challenge — food fixes both (keeps people in, raises spend per head) `[11]`.
