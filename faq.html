Frequently asked questions

Everything UK Thai restaurant operators ask us — wages, food costs, delivery platforms, chef visas, and more.

WAGES & NATIONAL LIVING WAGE
  • What is the National Living Wage rate from April 2026?
    The NLW rises to £12.71/hour for workers aged 21 and over from 1 April 2026. The 18-20 rate increases to £10.00/hour, and the Apprentice rate to £7.55/hour. Use our NLW Wage Calculator to see exactly how much this will cost your restaurant.
  • How much will the April 2026 NLW rise cost my Thai restaurant?
    For a typical restaurant with 3 full-time staff (21+) and 2 part-time staff (18-20), the increase is approximately £145-160/week or £7,500-8,300/year including NI. The exact figure depends on your staff mix, hours, and current pay rates. Calculate yours in 60 seconds with our Wage Calculator.
  • Do I have to pay the NLW to all my staff?
    Yes — the National Living Wage is a legal minimum. All workers aged 21+ must receive at least £12.71/hr from April 2026. Under-21s have lower minimum rates but must still be paid at least the applicable minimum wage for their age group. Failure to comply can result in HMRC fines and public naming.
FOOD COSTS & MARGINS
  • What is the average food cost percentage for a Thai restaurant in the UK?
    Most UK Thai restaurants operate with a food cost of 28-33% of menu price. Top-performing restaurants target 25-28% through strategic sourcing and menu engineering. Delivery tends to run higher due to packaging costs and platform commissions eating into net revenue. See the Menu Pricing Guide for detailed benchmarks.
  • What is a good GP margin for a Thai restaurant?
    A healthy gross profit margin for a UK Thai restaurant is 67-72% (food cost 28-33%). Net profit margins are typically 8-15% after rent, staff, utilities, and marketing. Our Profit Margin Defender tool can help you model where your margins sit.
  • How much should I charge for Pad Thai in 2026?
    Based on our Menu Price Index tracking 75 dishes across 5 UK cities, the average Pad Thai (chicken) costs £12.50 dine-in and £14.95 delivery. London averages £14.00/£16.50, while regional cities average £11.00/£13.50. Check our Menu Price Index for full pricing by dish and city.
DELIVERY PLATFORMS
  • How much does Uber Eats, Deliveroo, and Just Eat charge Thai restaurants?
    Commission rates vary significantly: Just Eat charges ~14% + £0.50 per order, while Deliveroo and Uber Eats charge 25-30%. On a £30 order, Just Eat takes £4.70 while Deliveroo/Uber take £7.50-£9.00. Factoring in VAT on commission, the real cost is even higher. Use our Delivery Profit Calculator to compare platforms side by side.
  • Should I be on all three delivery platforms?
    Not necessarily. Our data shows most Thai restaurants in the UK do 60-70% of their delivery volume through one primary platform. Being on all three spreads your orders thin and increases operational complexity. We recommend starting with Just Eat (lowest commission, widest reach) and adding others only if demand justifies it. See our Delivery Commissions Guide.
OPENING A THAI RESTAURANT
  • How much does it cost to open a Thai restaurant in the UK?
    Opening costs typically range from £80,000-£180,000 depending on location, size, and whether you're taking over an existing unit. Key costs include: fit-out (£30k-£80k), commercial kitchen equipment including wok stations (£15k-£35k), initial stock and licensing (£5k-£10k), and working capital (£20k-£50k). Our comprehensive Opening Guide covers every step.
  • Do I need a specific visa to hire Thai chefs?
    Yes — Thai chefs typically enter under the Skilled Worker Visa (RQF-6 equivalent). Your restaurant needs to be a Home Office approved sponsor. The process takes 8-16 weeks and costs approximately £3,000-£6,000 per chef including visa fees, immigration health surcharge, and legal costs. See our Thai Chef Visa Guide and Visa Assistant tool.
  • What BTU rating do I need for a Thai wok station?
    Authentic Thai cooking requires wok burners rated at 80,000-120,000 BTU — significantly higher than standard restaurant hobs (15,000-30,000 BTU). You'll also need an extraction canopy rated for high-heat wok cooking. This is a commonly overlooked cost that can add £8,000-£15,000 to your kitchen fit-out. Covered in detail in our Opening Guide.
MARKET DATA
  • How many Thai restaurants are there in the UK?
    There are 1,594 Thai restaurants across 253 UK cities and towns, based on Food Standards Agency data (updated June 2026). London has the most with 411, followed by Leeds (34) and Manchester (28). For the full breakdown, see our UK Thai Restaurant Count and City Breakdown.
  • Which UK city has the most Thai restaurants per capita?
    By density per 100,000 residents: Brighton leads (4.7), followed by Bournemouth (4.6), London (4.6), Edinburgh (4.4), and Manchester (4.9). Interestingly, some smaller cities like Bournemouth and Brighton punch well above their weight in Thai restaurant density. Our State of UK Thai Food 2026 report has full data.
  • Where are the underserved markets for Thai restaurants?
    Based on our analysis, cities with significant populations but relatively few Thai restaurants include Birmingham (17 restaurants, ~1.15M population), Sheffield (12, ~570K), and Leicester (10, ~370K). These markets show potential for new openings. Use our Where to Open Tool for a full analysis.
