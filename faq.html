What is ThaiData?

ThaiData is a UK‑wide Thai restaurant directory built from official Food Standards Agency (FSA) data, combined with our own city, chain, and pricing analysis. We focus on accuracy and coverage rather than reviews, so hygiene ratings and restaurant listings come from real inspection records and structured market analysis. The goal is to give diners, operators, and journalists a clean, up‑to‑date view of the UK Thai restaurant market — by city, by brand, and by price point — without hype or guesswork.

Hygiene Ratings & FSA Data

What Food Standards Agency hygiene scores mean, how Thai restaurants are rated, and why it matters. Every hygiene score on ThaiData comes directly from the FSA public register.
  • What is a Food Standards Agency (FSA) hygiene rating?
    The FSA hygiene rating scheme scores restaurants from 0 (urgent improvement needed) to 5 (very good). Ratings are based on three criteria: hygienic food handling (preparation, cooking, storage), structural condition of the premises (cleanliness, layout, facilities), and confidence in management (track record, documented procedures).

    Businesses in Wales and Northern Ireland are legally required to display their rating; in England and Scotland it is voluntary but the vast majority of Thai restaurants participate.
  • How are UK Thai restaurants rated for hygiene overall?
    Most UK Thai restaurants achieve strong hygiene ratings. Across all 1,594 FSA-registered Thai businesses, the majority hold 4 or 5 stars. Five-star-rated restaurants are concentrated in London, Leeds, and Manchester.

    You can check the hygiene rating of any specific restaurant on its restaurant page, sourced directly from the FSA register. Each listing shows the overall score, inspection date, and breakdown scores for hygiene, structural, and management criteria.
  • Why should restaurant operators care about hygiene scores?
    Hygiene ratings are publicly visible on the FSA website, Google Business Profiles, and delivery platforms like Just Eat and Uber Eats. A low score can directly impact customer trust and order volume. For operators, maintaining a 5-star rating means:
    • Better visibility on delivery platforms that filter by rating
    • Customer confidence — 72% of UK diners check hygiene ratings before ordering
    • Fewer inspections — consistently high-rated businesses may receive fewer re-inspections
    The Data Quality page details how scores map between England/Wales/Scotland systems.

Menu Pricing & Profitability

  • What is the average food cost percentage for a Thai restaurant in the UK?
    Most UK Thai restaurants operate with a food cost of 28-33% of menu price. Top-performing restaurants target 25-28% through strategic sourcing and menu engineering. Delivery tends to run higher due to packaging costs and platform commissions eating into net revenue.

    A healthy gross profit margin for a UK Thai restaurant is 67-72% (equivalent to 28-33% food cost). Net profit margins are typically 8-15% after rent, staff, utilities, and marketing.

Delivery Platforms

What delivery platforms charge, which ones to use, and how to stay profitable. The Delivery Commissions Guide covers platform strategy and the Delivery Profit Calculator models real costs.
  • How much do delivery platforms charge Thai restaurants?
    Commission rates vary significantly by platform: Just Eat charges approximately 14% + £0.50 per order, while Deliveroo and Uber Eats charge 25-30%. On a £30 order, Just Eat takes about £4.70 while Deliveroo and Uber Eats take £7.50 to £9.00. Factoring in VAT on commission, the real cost is even higher.

    Use the Delivery Profit Calculator to compare platforms side by side. The Delivery Profit Analysis report has more data.
  • Should my Thai restaurant be on all three delivery platforms?
    Not necessarily. Our data shows most Thai restaurants do 60-70% of delivery volume through one primary platform. Being on all three spreads orders thin and increases operational complexity. A common approach is starting with Just Eat (lowest commission, widest reach) and adding others only if demand justifies it.

    The Delivery Commissions Guide covers platform strategy and commission negotiation in detail.

Dine-in vs Takeaway

How the UK Thai restaurant market splits between eat-in and takeaway, and what it means for operators and diners. Browse restaurants by type or check the city breakdown for local splits.
  • What is the typical dine-in vs takeaway split for UK Thai restaurants?
    Among the 1,594 FSA-registered Thai businesses, approximately 53% are dine-in restaurants and 47% are takeaway-only. This split varies significantly by region:
    • London: 71% dine-in, 29% takeaway — the capital skews heavily toward sit-down dining
    • Regional cities: roughly balanced, with some cities like Leeds and Manchester having more even splits
    • Smaller towns: tend toward takeaway-only operations
    The City-by-City Breakdown includes type-split data for every UK city with Thai restaurants.
  • Should I offer both dine-in and takeaway?
    Many successful Thai restaurants operate a hybrid model — dine-in for evening meals and takeaway/delivery for lunch and late orders. Key considerations include:
    • Kitchen capacity: a single kitchen can handle both, but ticket management matters during peak hours
    • Menu pricing: most restaurants charge 20-23% more on delivery to offset platform commissions
    • Space requirements: takeaway-only operations need less front-of-house space, reducing rent costs

Data Sourcing & Methodology

Where ThaiData gets its restaurant information, how often it is updated, and what limitations apply. See the Data Quality page for a full transparency report.
  • Where does ThaiData get its restaurant data?
    ThaiData sources all restaurant listings from the UK Food Standards Agency — specifically the Food Hygiene Rating Scheme (FHRS) for England, Wales, and Northern Ireland, and the Food Hygiene Information Scheme (FHIS) for Scotland. Restaurant names, addresses, hygiene scores, and inspection dates are pulled directly from these official public registers via the FSA API.

    We identify Thai restaurants by searching for Thai-related keywords in business names and filtering by cuisine type where available. The Data Quality page explains the methodology in full.
  • How often is the restaurant data updated?
    The restaurant database is refreshed weekly from the Food Standards Agency API. Each restaurant page shows its last inspection date and the date of the most recent ThaiData update. The FSA data itself is updated as local authorities conduct inspections, so there is usually a lag of several weeks between an inspection and it appearing on ThaiData.

    Current data: June 2026. Chain location counts are based on FSA register name matching and may not reflect a chain's full estate.
  • How reliable is the information on ThaiData?
    Restaurant names, addresses, and hygiene scores come directly from the FSA public register, making them highly reliable for FSA-registered businesses. However, ThaiData does not independently verify:
    • Whether a business is actively trading (closures and openings happen between FSA updates)
    • Whether a restaurant primarily serves Thai food (we use name-based detection)
    • Operating hours, menu prices, or contact details (these come from restaurant websites when available)
    The Data Quality page provides full detail on limitations and known issues.
  • How do I claim or update my ThaiData listing?
    If you own or manage a Thai restaurant in the UK, you can claim your listing for free. Go to your restaurant's page and click "Claim This Listing", or visit /claim/ and search for your restaurant. Once verified, you'll be able to update your details, add photos, menus, opening hours, website and social links, all at no cost.

    If you can't find your restaurant or someone else has claimed it, use the "Can't find your restaurant?" link on the claim page or email hello@thaidata.uk and we'll help.

Opening & Operating

Practical information for starting and running a Thai restaurant in the UK. The Opening Guide covers everything from business plans to kitchen fit-out.
  • How much does it cost to open a Thai restaurant in the UK?
    Opening costs typically range from £80,000 to £180,000 depending on location, size, and whether you are taking over an existing unit. Key cost categories:
    • Fit-out: £30k-£80k (seating, decor, signage)
    • Commercial kitchen: £15k-£35k including wok stations and extraction
    • Initial stock & licensing: £5k-£10k
    • Working capital: £20k-£50k (3-6 months runway)
    The Opening Guide covers every step from business plan templates to supplier lists. See also the Opening Timeline for typical milestones.
  • Do I need a specific visa to hire Thai chefs?
    Thai chefs typically enter the UK under the Skilled Worker Visa (RQF-6 equivalent). Your restaurant must be a Home Office approved sponsor. The process takes 8-16 weeks and costs approximately £3,000-£6,000 per chef including visa fees, immigration health surcharge, and legal costs.

    See the Thai Chef Visa Guide for a step-by-step sponsorship walkthrough, and the Visa Assistant tool to estimate costs.
  • What BTU rating do I need for a Thai wok station?
    Authentic Thai cooking requires wok burners rated at 80,000-120,000 BTU — significantly higher than standard restaurant hobs (15,000-30,000 BTU). You will also need an extraction canopy rated for high-heat wok cooking. This is a commonly overlooked cost that can add £8,000-£15,000 to your kitchen fit-out.

    Covered in detail in the Opening Guide, along with wholesale supplier recommendations for kitchen equipment.
  • What is the NLW rate from April 2026 and how does it affect Thai restaurants?
    The National Living Wage rises to £12.71/hour for workers aged 21+ from 1 April 2026. The 18-20 rate goes to £10.00/hour, and the Apprentice rate to £7.55/hour. For a typical Thai restaurant with 3 full-time and 2 part-time staff, this represents an increase of approximately £145-160/week or £7,500-8,300/year including NI.

    Use the NLW Wage Calculator on the homepage to model your exact cost. The NLW Impact Report has industry-wide analysis.
