Overview

"Taste of Thai" is not a formal UK restaurant chain. Rather, it is a commonly used trading name adopted by multiple independent Thai restaurants operating across different regions of the UK. These establishments have no apparent corporate ownership, franchise relationship, or shared business structure. Each restaurant operates autonomously with its own ownership, website (where present), menu development, and local supply chains.

The most prominent independently operated "Taste of Thai" restaurants include: Taste of Thai in Gloucester (117-119 Southgate Street, GL1 1UT, www.taste-of-thai.co.uk), which holds a 5/5 food hygiene rating as of March 2025; Taste of Thai in Ashby de la Zouch, Leicestershire (84-86 Market Street, LE65 1AP), whose limited company "TASTE OF THAI ASHBY LIMITED" was incorporated in February 2024 and dissolved in May 2025; Taste of Thai in Fakenham, Norfolk (39 Market Place, NR21 9BY), which has been operating for 6+ years and holds a 5/5 hygiene rating as of March 2025; and Taste of Thai in Seaton, Devon (18 Fore Street, EX12 2LA), opened in 2020 by local residents Steve Carter and his wife Kee, who started as a home takeaway operation during lockdown before expanding to a 22-seater restaurant — this location also holds a "Good" hygiene rating across all categories as of July 2025. Additional registrations under the "Taste of Thai" name exist on the Food Standards Agency database, including one at The Huntsman pub in Thurlstone, South Yorkshire.

All of these restaurants position themselves as authentic Thai cuisine establishments, employing fully trained Thai chefs and offering traditional dishes such as Tom Yum soup, Thai green/red curries, Pad Thai, and stir-fry dishes. Pricing across the locations falls in the mid-range bracket (mains approximately £10-£16), consistent with independent casual-dining Thai restaurants in UK market towns. The repeated use of the "Taste of Thai" name likely reflects its intuitive descriptiveness rather than any organised brand strategy. Prospective diners should evaluate each location on its own merits, as there is no shared quality standard, menu, or management structure linking them.
