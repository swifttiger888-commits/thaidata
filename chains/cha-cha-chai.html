Overview

Cha Cha Chai is a UK-based South Asian street food and chai cafe chain founded in Bradford, West Yorkshire in 2017/2018 by Nasar Nabi. Despite its name potentially suggesting Thai cuisine, the chain serves authentic South Asian (Indian/Pakistani) food and beverages. The business began as a single location in a shipping container with a makeshift drive-through and has since expanded to over 28 locations across England through a franchise model. The chain is headquartered in Bradford with a central production facility where chai is prepared in large traditional pots (deghs) overnight and distributed to stores daily.

The menu is extensive and affordable, featuring over 100 items including breakfast (Full English, desi omelettes, parathas, halwa puri), burgers, street food (samosas, seekh kebabs, pani puri, wraps), mains (biryani, curries), a wide range of desserts (donuts, cookie dough, brownies, milk cakes, falooda, gulab jamun), and a variety of hot and cold drinks centred around their signature karak chai and Kashmiri pink tea. Most items are priced under £15, making it an accessible casual dining option.

The chain differentiates itself through its drive-through format, 24-hour licensing at select locations, and focus on authentic desi chai culture. According to a 2022 Hyphen Online feature, there were 7 branches in Bradford and 17 franchised locations across northern England, with 11 more scheduled to open that year. The Food Standards Agency data from 2026 shows 28 rated UK locations. The average food hygiene rating of 3.7/5 is below the national average of 4.5, with only 29% of locations holding a 5-star rating. The brand actively seeks franchise partners and has positioned itself as a growing player in the UK's booming desi chai cafe market.

Important Note

Cha Cha Chai is not a Thai restaurant chain. It is a South Asian (Indian/Pakistani) street food and chai cafe. The name "Cha Cha Chai" refers to "chai" (tea) in South Asian languages, not Thai cuisine. Menu items include parathas, biryanis, samosas, seekh kebabs, and desi-style teas - not Thai dishes.
