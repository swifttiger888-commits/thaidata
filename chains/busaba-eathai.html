Overview

Busaba (formerly Busaba Eathai) is a Thai casual dining restaurant chain founded in London in 1999 by Alan Yau, the Hong Kong-born British restaurateur who also created Wagamama, Hakkasan, and Yauatcha. The chain currently operates 6 locations across London, primarily in high-footfall areas including Westfield Stratford City, Covent Garden, Westfield London (Shepherd's Bush), St Christopher's Place (Marylebone), Soho (Wardour Street), and The O2 Arena in Greenwich.

The chain positions itself as "modern Bangkok eating in the heart of London," with a menu designed for sharing that includes freshly prepared salads, Pad Thais, curries, and other Thai dishes. A distinctive feature of Busaba is its philosophical underpinning: the ethos of "Sookjai" (meaning "happy heart" in Thai), based on traditional Buddhist values of mindful living and enjoyment. The chain also emphasises plant-based offerings, with a large portion of its menu being vegetarian or vegan. The restaurant's name references the Thai greeting "sawatdee" and aims to create an accessible, communal dining experience.

The parent company, Busaba Eathai Limited (company number 04956194), entered administration on 16 July 2025, with administrators Alex David Cadwallader and Neil A Bennett appointed. The business was sold out of administration via a pre-pack arrangement to Seaco Investments on 25 July 2025. Several related companies within the group were dissolved in 2023, including Busaba Eathai Holdings Limited, Busaba Eathai Acquisitions Limited, and Busaba Eathai (Store Street) Limited. All 6 locations continue to operate under the Busaba brand.
