Massaman (แกงมัสมั่น)

The curry CNN ranked the world's best food. Nutmeg, mace, cardamom, cinnamon and cloves pounded into a paste, coconut cream cracked, potatoes and tamarind. Not peanut sauce — ever.

By Johnny Tiger · Published 26 September 2026 · Serves 10 (halves well) · ~80 minutes

The Curry Kings Wrote About

Massaman is Thai cuisine's Persian thread. The name comes from "Muslim" — the dish entered Siam through Persian and Indian traders and was refined in the royal kitchens. Its oldest endorsement is literary: King Rama II's nineteenth-century food poem sings of "massaman, a curry of my beloved" — fragrant with cumin, fierce in heat. Two centuries later, CNN Travel ranked it the #1 food in the world (2021), and in 2022 Chef Chumpol Jangprai of R-HAAN (2 Michelin stars) served his massaman to the 21 world leaders of the APEC summit gala.

The dish's identity is its spice arsenal — nutmeg, mace, cardamom, cinnamon, cloves, cumin and coriander seed, dry-roasted and pounded with the chillies and aromatics. Western versions flatten this into "mild red curry with peanut butter." That is not this dish.

The Method

  1. Fry the aromatics. Lemongrass, shallots, galangal, garlic in a little oil until golden; the deseeded dried chillies fried crisp. The paste's depth starts here.
  2. Roast the spices. Nutmeg, mace, cardamom, coriander seeds, cumin, cinnamon, cloves — dry pan until fragrant. This is the identity of the dish; skip none of them.
  3. Pound everything with the shrimp paste and salt until very fine. A fine paste means a curry that cracks beautifully and holds no fibres.
  4. Crack the cream. Part of the coconut cream over medium-low until the oil separates, then fry the paste in it until fragrant.
  5. Season, then boil. Palm sugar, tamarind water, fish sauce stirred until dissolved, the rest of the cream in, bring to a boil.
  6. Chicken in with the cardamom pods and bay leaves, coconut milk over, medium simmer 25–30 minutes.
  7. Potatoes and onions in, 20–30 minutes until tender — don't let the potatoes disintegrate.
  8. Peanuts last. Roasted peanuts in, a brief simmer, taste: a touch of sour, rounded sweet, salt to finish. Jasmine rice on the side.

The Beef Variant (Pim's Kitchen, the braise-first way)

Braise first: 1 kg of beef plate in 4 cups coconut milk with a little salt, low and patient — 1 hour 10 minutes to 1½ hours until tender. Then fry 200 g of paste in cracked coconut cream, add the beef, sweet potatoes (600 g), quartered onions (200 g), roasted peanuts (80 g) and 6 roasted cardamom pods.

Balance: 200 g coconut sugar, 100 g tamarind water, fish sauce and salt — Pim's own balance is a light sour leading, rounded sweet, salt at the finish, and she notes taste is personal: some like salt to lead, some sweet, some sour.

Finish: simmer 30 minutes until the onions soften and sweet potatoes are tender, then pour over the remaining coconut cream and bring back to the boil.

Where Thai Sources Disagree

Protein. Chicken, beef, duck, lamb, even tofu — all standard. Beef is the traditional grand version; chicken is the everyday one.

Potato vs sweet potato. Both are used; sweet potato is the southern/home alternative.

Peanuts vs cashews. Thai Wikipedia lists both; peanuts are the commoner.

Star anise. Some home versions add it; others (and most shop pastes) skip it.

The balance. Pim is explicit that the sour-sweet-salty order is personal preference — no single canonical balance exists.

How Western Versions Get It Wrong

  • Peanut sauce. The defining western error. Real massaman is a spice curry — nutmeg, mace, cardamom, cinnamon, cloves — with roasted peanuts added at the end as a nut, never as the sauce. If it tastes of peanut butter, it isn't massaman.
  • "Mild red curry." Massaman paste is its own recipe. Substituting red curry paste plus peanut butter makes a different dish.
  • Skimped spices. The dry-roasted spice layer is the dish. Omit it and you have sweet coconut soup.
  • No tamarind. The sour note is what keeps the richness in check.
  • Mushy potatoes. Potatoes go in late and come out tender, not dissolved into the sauce.
  • Over-sweetened. The balance is sour-touched, sweet-rounded, salt-finished — not a dessert curry.

UK Ingredient Notes

  • Whole nutmeg, mace, cardamom pods, cinnamon, cloves, cumin and coriander seeds: any good spice aisle or South Asian grocer. Whole spices, dry-roasted, beat pre-ground every time.
  • Dried spur and bird's eye chillies, shrimp paste, makrut zest, palm sugar, tamarind: Asian grocers. Tamarind block soaked in hot water = tamarind water.
  • Chicken thighs over breast — the long simmer stays tender. For beef: brisket or chuck, braised as in the variant.
  • Waxy potatoes hold their shape better than floury ones.
  • The paste freezes well — make double, freeze half, and massaman becomes a weeknight curry.
