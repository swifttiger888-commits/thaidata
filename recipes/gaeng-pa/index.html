Gaeng Pa (แกงป่า)

The jungle curry — no coconut milk, no mercy. Chilli-hot water broth loaded with vegetables and wild herbs, finished with green peppercorns.

By Johnny Tiger · Published 27 September 2026 · Serves 2 · ~30 minutes

The Curry That Refused the Coconut

แกงป่า — "jungle curry" — is the Thai curry made for the forest, where coconuts don't grow. Where every other Thai curry rounds itself with coconut milk, gaeng pa runs on water and the paste alone, so the heat arrives undiluted. The Zabwer source puts the target plainly: "it has to be hot enough that you eat it wiping sweat — that's when it's good. If you don't like heat, reduce the chillies."

The Aroifin method is a lesson in ordering: the paste is stirred into boiling water, the chicken goes in to cook through, then the vegetables join in order of their cooking time — pumpkin and pea aubergines first, baby corn, bamboo and long beans after — so everything lands tender at the same moment. Seasoning is just fish sauce and a little palm sugar; the dish doesn't want balance, it wants heat.

The finish is the dish's real signature: green peppercorns on the stem, torn kaffir lime leaves, fingerroot and holy basil go in last, one stir, off the heat — the source's rule — so the fresh aromatics hit the nose before the chilli hits the tongue. Served over rice, or over khanom jeen rice noodles for the central-Thai way.

The Method

  1. Paste into the boil. Water at a hard boil, gaeng pa paste stirred in until it disperses.
  2. Chicken, then vegetables in order. Chicken until just cooked; pumpkin and pea aubergines first, then baby corn, bamboo, long beans and aubergine clusters — each in its own cooking-time slot.
  3. Season. Fish sauce and palm sugar.
  4. Herbs last. Green peppercorns, spur chilli, torn kaffir, fingerroot and holy basil — one stir, off the heat. Serve with hot rice or khanom jeen.

Where Thai Sources Disagree

The paste. Aroifin builds from 2 tablespoons of ready gaeng pa paste; Phol Food Mafia pounds from scratch — 9 dried chillies, shrimp paste, kaffir zest and the usual roots. The water-based broth depends entirely on the paste, so the paste decides the curry.

To fry the paste or not. The standard method fries the paste in oil before the water goes in; the Blockdit grandmother's version (รสมือย่า) never fries it — no oil slick on the surface, "but it's still ferociously hot." Two schools of the same curry.

To season or not. Phol's recipe is adapted from a แม่บ้านบุษราคัม school recipe whose original version doesn't season with fish sauce and sugar at all — he added the sweet-salt balance himself. The heritage version trusts the paste and shrimp paste alone.

How many vegetables. Aroifin's is a restrained set; Zabwer's is the full mixed-vegetable version — "a curry full of fibre" — with whatever the market has. Both are gaeng pa; the forest decides the vegetables.

Chicken cut. Aroifin specifies thigh — "tenderer than breast, and it doesn't dry out when simmered." The home recipes use whatever is in the fridge.

How hot. Zabwer demands sweat-wiping heat and offers the chilli reduction as the only concession. The curry's identity is the burn.

How Western Versions Get It Wrong

  • Adding coconut milk. Coconut milk makes it a different curry entirely. The water base is the dish's definition.
  • Red curry paste as a substitute. Gaeng pa paste is hotter and herbal; red paste gives a sweeter, roasted, coconut-style flavour.
  • Throwing all the vegetables in at once. The staggered order is the method — everything lands at the same tenderness.
  • Omitting the green peppercorns. The fresh peppercorn stems are the signature finish, not decoration.
  • Sweetness. Two teaspoons of palm sugar is the ceiling. A sweet jungle curry has been tamed into something else.

UK Ingredient Notes

  • Gaeng pa paste: tinned or sachet at Thai grocers — "nam prik gaeng pa".
  • Pea aubergines (มะเขือเปราะ) and clusters (มะเขือพวง): Thai grocers, fresh. The clusters are the tiny green balls; leave them whole.
  • Green peppercorns on the stem: Thai grocers sell them fresh in brine or frozen.
  • Holy basil (กะเพรา): Thai grocers — not sweet basil.
  • Fingerroot (กระชาย): Thai grocers; the thin knobby roots, sliced thin.
  • Serve with jasmine rice or khanom jeen rice noodles.
