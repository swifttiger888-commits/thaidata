Kanom Jeeb (Pork and Prawn Dumplings)

Steamed Thai dim sum dumplings — a bouncy pork and prawn filling wrapped in thin wonton skins and pleated open at the top. The dumpling that anchors every Thai starter menu.

By Johnny Tiger · Published 3 October 2026 · Serves 4 · 1 hour

ขนมจีบ

Kanom jeeb is the steamed one. Where gyoza is pan-fried with a crisp base and kiao tod is deep-fried, kanom jeeb goes into the steamer — a cup-shaped pleat of thin wonton skin, open at the top so you can see the filling, brushed with fried garlic oil until it glistens.

Thai dim sum carts sell it in bamboo baskets alongside ha-gaw and salapao, and nearly every Thai restaurant in the UK lists it in the starter section. The filling is the point of difference from its Chinese relatives: coriander root, garlic and white pepper pounded into a paste, worked into pork and prawn with starch until the texture turns springy — the quality Thais call คว่าเด้ง, "bounce".

The Recipe (serves 4 · ~1 hr)

Allergens: gluten, shellfish, soy, sesame · Suitable for: dairy-free

For the filling

หมูบดMinced pork, with some fat300g
กุ้งPrawns, shelled, finely chopped150g
รากผักชีCoriander root2 roots
กระเทียมGarlic3 cloves
พริกไทยขาวWhite peppercorns1/2 tsp
ซีอิ๊วขาวLight soy sauce1 tbsp
ซอสปรุงรสSeasoning sauce1 tbsp
น้ำตาลทรายSugar1 tbsp
น้ำมันงาSesame oil1 tsp
แป้งมันTapioca or corn starch1 tbsp
แครอทCarrot, finely diced, for the filling and to top each dumpling50g

To assemble

ThaiEnglishAmount
แผ่นเกี๊ยวบางThin wonton wrappers24
น้ำมันเจียวกระเทียมFried garlic oil, for brushing2 tbsp

For the dipping sauce

ThaiEnglishAmount
ซีอิ๊วดำDark soy sauce1/4 cup
น้ำเชื่อมSugar syrup2 tbsp
น้ำส้มสายชูWhite vinegar1 tbsp
พริกจินดาFresh bird's eye chillies, finely pounded1 tbsp

The Method

  1. Make the filling. Pound the coriander root, garlic and white pepper into a paste (three friends / sam kler). Mix with the pork, chopped prawns, soy, seasoning sauce, sugar, sesame oil and starch. Knead hard in one direction until the mixture turns dense and slightly springy. Chill for 30 minutes — the bouncy texture depends on cold, worked meat.
  2. Fill and pleat. Trim the corners off the wrappers to round them. Holding a wrapper in a loose fist, add a teaspoon of filling. Gather the edges upward and squeeze gently so the top stays open, leaving a little filling visible. Dab a few pieces of carrot on top.
  3. Steam. Line a steamer with banana leaf or oiled paper and arrange the dumplings with space between them. Steam over boiling water for 5–10 minutes until the filling is cooked through. Brush with fried garlic oil and steam one minute more.
  4. Serve. Serve hot with the dark soy dipping sauce.

The bounce

Cold is the secret. Chill the mince before you work it, knead in one direction until it turns dense and slightly tacky, then rest it in the fridge. Warm or over-handled filling steams up crumbly instead of springy.

The open-top pleat

Kanom jeeb is not sealed shut. You gather the wrapper loosely around the filling so it sits like a cup, leaving the top open — partly for looks, partly so the steam can cook the filling directly. A pinch of diced carrot on top is the traditional garnish.

Serving

  • Sauce: Dark soy, sugar syrup, vinegar and pounded chillies stirred together

Cook's Tips

  • The 'bounce' (ความเด้ง) of a good kanom jeeb comes from working the mince with starch and keeping everything cold — never warm the filling before steaming.
  • If the wrappers dry out while you work, cover them with a damp cloth.
  • Kanom jeeb freeze well raw; steam straight from the freezer with three extra minutes.

Variations

  • Prawn-first version. The Hong Kong-style recipe uses 800g prawns to 200g pork fat and adds baking soda for extra spring.
  • Bird dumpling (kanom jeeb tua nok). A northern sweet version with a steamed rice-flour skin and a palm-sugar and peanut filling.
