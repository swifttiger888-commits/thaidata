Thai Lemon Iced Tea (ชามะนาว)

Thai tea brewed dark, sweetened, and cut with fresh lime over a full glass of ice. Cha yen without the milk — sharp where its cousin is creamy.

By Johnny Tiger · Published 27 September 2026 · Makes 1 glass · ~10 minutes

Cha Yen Without the Milk

ชามะนาว — "tea with lime" — is the second drink on every Thai stall's board, the one for people who find cha yen too rich for the afternoon. The Kapook source calls it exactly what it is: "เครื่องดื่มสุดคลาสสิกที่หลายคนสั่งตามคาเฟ่หรือร้านรถเข็น" — the classic ordered at cafés and cart stalls alike. Same deeply brewed Thai tea, same glass of ice; the condensed milk is swapped for fresh lime juice, and the drink flips from creamy-sweet to sharp and clean.

The tea is the same ผงชาไทย, Thai tea powder — the Pantip home-cooks asking for the recipe name ชาตรามือ, the Cha Tra Mue brand, as the default; the Kapook source allows red tea powder as the alternative. Brewed strong through a sock, strained back and forth until dark, rested two or three minutes — the same discipline as cha yen, because weak tea collapses the moment the lime hits it.

Two details carry the drink. The pinch of salt in the sweetening — the Kapook source's trick that rounds the sugar and stops the tea tasting flat — and the lime going in while the tea is still warm, so the juice and sugar dissolve into one syrup rather than sitting in layers. The source's cost note for stall owners: lime powder can stand in for fresh fruit when limes are expensive.

The Recipe (one 16 oz glass — the Kapook make-to-sell version)

ThaiEnglishAmount
ผงชาไทยหรือผงชาแดงThai tea powder (or red tea powder)3 tbsp
น้ำร้อนจัดBoiling water120 ml
น้ำตาลทรายSugar2 tbsp
เกลือปลายช้อนชาSalt, a pincha pinch
น้ำมะนาวFresh lime juice1–1½ limes
น้ำแข็งIce1 glass (16 oz)
ถุงชงชาTea sock or tea bag1

The Method

  1. Brew dark. Tea powder into the sock or bag, boiling water through, dipping and straining back and forth until the colour is deep. Rest 2–3 minutes.
  2. Sweeten warm. Sugar and the pinch of salt into a heatproof jug; hot tea over; stir until fully dissolved.
  3. Lime in, ice over. Stir the fresh lime juice through, taste the sweet-sour line, then pour over a full glass of ice.

Where Thai Sources Disagree

Thai tea powder vs plain black tea. Kapook brews from Thai tea powder first, red tea powder as the alternative; the Pantip café-owner thread asks for both ชาดำเย็น (sweet iced black tea) and ชามะนาว side by side — the same drink can be built on either leaf. The Cha Tra Mue powder gives the deeper colour and the vanilla edge.

Fresh lime vs lime powder. Kapook's stall economics: fresh limes for flavour, powdered lime when the price spikes. The source publishes both as the same drink, cost being the deciding variable.

Lime in the pot or in the glass. Kapook dissolves the lime into the warm sweetened tea before icing. Other stalls squeeze at serving; the sources don't settle it — only Kapook's order of operations is on record.

The salt. Kapook's pinch is the make-to-sell touch. The home threads rarely mention it; the drink stands without it, but the salt rounds the sugar in the stall version.

How Western Versions Get It Wrong

  • Calling it an Arnold Palmer. The Thai drink is built on brewed Thai tea powder — dark, malty, vanilla-edged — not light iced tea.
  • Lime cordial. The drink is fresh lime juice over strong tea. Cordial makes it lime-flavoured squash.
  • Weak tea. Brewed pale, the lime flattens it to brown water. The sources strain the tea until dark.
  • Adding milk. Milk plus lime curdles — and it's also just cha yen, a different drink. ชามะนาว is dairy-free by definition.
  • Lemon instead of lime. Thai มะนาว is the small green-yellow lime — sharper, more fragrant than lemon, and it's the name of the drink.

UK Ingredient Notes

  • Thai tea powder: the Cha Tra Mue (ชาตรามือ) red can, at Thai grocers or online — it's the brand the Thai threads name.
  • Limes: UK limes are the Persian type; two make the drink right. The small Thai มะนาว is rarely sold here.
  • Tea sock: a reusable cloth tea sock from Thai grocers, or a large fillable tea bag — the powder is fine, so paper teabags clog.
  • Ice: the glass should be full of it — the drink is meant to dilute slowly as you drink.
  • Serve with som tum or anything fried — the stall pairing is savoury-spicy food and this glass.
