Nam Tok (น้ำตก)

น้ำตก means "waterfall" — the juices that drip when the grilled meat comes off the fire. Pork neck sliced warm, dressed in its own drippings with lime, fish sauce, toasted rice and mint.

By Johnny Tiger · Published 27 September 2026 · Serves 2 · ~40 minutes

The Salad Named for the Waterfall

The Isaan kitchen's name for it is poetic and exact: น้ำตก — waterfall. When the grilled meat is lifted off the fire and sliced, the juices run — and those drippings, mixed with the lime and fish sauce, are the dressing. That's the whole dish in one image: a salad whose sauce is the meat's own.

Nam tok is the grilled sibling of laab. Laab is poached or stir-fried and minced; nam tok is grilled and sliced, with the fire's smoke in the dressing. Everything else is family: toasted rice powder (ข้าวคั่ว) for the nutty depth, ground dried chillies for the heat, shallots, spring onion, sawtooth coriander and mint. It is eaten with sticky rice and raw cabbage, and it is not supposed to be polite.

The classic cut is สันคอหมู — pork neck, marbled enough to stay juicy over fire, and Thai cooks often marinate it first — evaporated milk, white soy, oyster sauce and coriander root, the same marinade family as moo ping. The beef version (น้ำตกเนื้อ) is the same dish with a beefier argument.

The Method

  1. Grill. The pork neck over charcoal, or a very hot grill pan, until charred at the edges and just cooked inside. Rest it briefly — the resting juices are the dressing.
  2. Slice warm. Across the grain, while it's still warm. Cold meat drinks the dressing instead of mixing with it.
  3. Dress. Toasted rice powder and ground chillies in first, then lime and fish sauce, tossed with the meat and its juices.
  4. Herbs in. Shallots, spring onion, coriander, sawtooth coriander and most of the mint, folded through.
  5. Taste. Sour first, salty behind it, the toastiness underneath. Adjust the lime and fish sauce against each other.
  6. Serve. Over shredded cabbage, cucumber beside it, sticky rice in hand, the last mint leaves on top.

Where Thai Sources Disagree

Fire or pan. The classic grills the meat and dresses it in the drippings alone; Kapook's published version skips the fire entirely and warms sliced pork through stock in a pan. Faster, but the smoke is the point.

Pork or beef. น้ำตกหมู (pork, usually neck) and น้ำตกเนื้อ (beef) are the same dish on different animals. Beef versions often carry a little more chilli.

The herbs. Mint is non-negotiable; sawtooth coriander (ผักชีฝรั่ง) is in most but not all; some recipes reach for Vietnamese coriander. The mint at minimum.

Marinade or not. Purists grill the neck bare and let the fire do the talking; the evaporated-milk-and-soy marinade (ครัวบ้านพิม's) makes it juicier and a touch sweeter.

What it's eaten with. Sticky rice and raw cabbage are the standard; cucumber appears in some households. Nobody argues with the sticky rice.

How Western Versions Get It Wrong

  • No fire, no drippings. The waterfall is the dish. Poached or pan-cooked meat dressed like a vinaigrette salad misses the entire point.
  • Skipping the toasted rice. ข้าวคั่ว is the nutty depth that holds the sharp dressing together. Leave it out and the salad shouts.
  • Sweet chilli dressing. Western "Thai beef salad" bottles pour sugar-led sauce over cold steak. Nam tok is sour-led, lime and fish sauce, nothing sweet at the front.
  • No mint. The mint is the freshness that makes the heat make sense.
  • Served cold. The meat should still be warm when it meets the dressing — the dish is built in the few minutes after the fire.

UK Ingredient Notes

  • Pork neck: any good butcher. It's the same cut as the collar roasts; ask for it in one piece to grill whole.
  • Toasted rice powder: make it — dry-toast raw jasmine rice in a pan until deep golden, grind fine. Thai grocers also sell it ready-made.
  • Ground dried chillies: Thai grocers' roasted chilli flakes beat the supermarket kind; the roast matters.
  • Sawtooth coriander: Thai grocers, fresh. If it's missing, the salad survives on coriander and mint — but it's worth finding.
  • Sticky rice: Thai grocers' glutinous rice, steamed — see the mango sticky rice page for the method.
  • Charcoal beats the grill pan, and both beat the oven — the smoke is the dressing's first ingredient.
