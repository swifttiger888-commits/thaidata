Pad Khao Pord Orn (Stir-Fried Baby Corn)

Stir-fried baby corn — crunchy young corn cobs wok-fried with your choice of prawns, pork or mushrooms in a light oyster-soy glaze. The quick, clean vegetable stir-fry that rounds out any Thai menu.

By Johnny Tiger · Published 3 October 2026 · Serves 2 · 15 minutes

ผัดข้าวโพดอ่อน

Pad khao pord orn is the workhorse of Thai home cooking — baby corn, a little protein, a quick oyster-soy glaze, five minutes over high heat. It is not a famous dish and no one photographs it, but it is on nearly every UK Thai menu because it is fast, cheap, and reliably good.

The dish is really a template. Thai cooks run it with prawns (kung), pork belly (sam chan), chicken or mushrooms, and the seasoning barely changes: oyster sauce, a splash of seasoning sauce, sugar, white pepper, garlic, spring onion.

The Method

  1. Prep. Blanch the prawns for one minute in boiling water if you want them plump rather than curled — or keep them raw for more flavour in the wok. Halve the baby corn on the diagonal and have everything else cut and ready before the wok goes on.
  2. Stir-fry. Heat the oil over medium-high. Fry the garlic until golden, then add the prawns and stir-fry until almost cooked. Add the baby corn, mushrooms and carrot. Season with oyster sauce, soy, sugar and pepper, with a splash of water to help the sauce coat. Keep the heat high and the vegetables moving — baby corn should stay crunchy, never steamed soft.
  3. Finish. Add the spring onions (and chillies if using) in the last thirty seconds. Serve immediately with steamed rice.

Crunch is the point

Baby corn is eaten for its snap. The entire cooking time is two or three minutes over high heat — enough to heat it through and glaze it, not enough to soften it. The moment it stops crunching, the dish has failed, which is why everything must be cut and ready before the wok gets hot.

The 1:1 rule

Across the Thai sources the sauce ratio repeats: oyster sauce and seasoning sauce one-to-one, plus sugar to round the salt. Everything else is negotiable; that balance is not.

Serving

  • Rice: Steamed jasmine rice
  • Extra: Squeeze of lime or prik nam pla for the table

Cook's Tips

  • Baby corn is done the moment it is hot through. Overcooking turns it from crunchy to squeaky to soft.
  • The sauce ratio that keeps repeating across Thai sources is oyster sauce to seasoning sauce at 1:1 — build from there.
  • This is a template stir-fry: swap the prawns for pork belly, chicken, squid or tofu and keep everything else the same.

Variations

  • Pork belly version. Wongnai's sam chan phat khao pod orn fries sliced pork belly until golden first, then stir-fries the corn in the rendered fat with fish sauce.
  • Vegetarian version. No meat at all — baby corn braised in a glossy mushroom-soy sauce thickened with tapioca starch.
