Khao Soi (ข้าวซอย)

Chiang Mai's curry noodle soup — the real version. Soft noodles, crisp noodles, cracked coconut cream, and a paste you pound yourself. Researched from Thai-language chef sources and translated; sources cited below.

By Johnny Tiger · Published 25 September 2026 · Serves 5 · ~65 minutes

Where It Comes From

Khao soi is Chiang Mai's signature dish. It arrived in Lanna with Chinese-Muslim traders from Yunnan — the Chin Haw — who carried it along the old caravan routes. Northern cooks rebuilt it with local ingredients: fresh turmeric, makrut lime, coconut cream — until it became something entirely its own. The beef version belongs to the Muslim khao soi tradition: shops like Khao Soi Pa Pai (Wat Ket, 40+ years) and the six-decade legend Khao Soi Samer Jai in Fa Ham still serve it. In 2022, TasteAtlas ranked khao soi the #1 soup in the world.

One bowl contains five textures: soft noodle, crisp noodle, tender chicken, silky broth, sharp pickle. That's the point of the dish — and it's the part most western versions drop.

Noodles & table condiments

  • เส้นข้าวซอย — flat egg noodles, 500 g: half blanched, half deep-fried
  • ผักกาดดอง — pickled mustard greens, sliced
  • หอมแดงซอย — raw shallot, sliced
  • มะนาวหั่นซีก — lime wedges
  • พริกแห้งคั่วน้ำมัน — dried chillies fried in oil
  • ต้นหอม / ผักชี — spring onion & coriander

The Method

  1. Roast. Dry-roast the chillies, lemongrass, shallots, garlic, cardamom and coriander seeds in a pan until fragrant.
  2. Pound. Pound the roasted aromatics with the salt, turmeric and curry powder until smooth, then add the makrut zest and shrimp paste last. A mortar and pestle matters — the paste should be fine, not chunky.
  3. Crack the cream. Heat the coconut cream over medium heat until it splits — oil beads on the surface. Don't rush this step.
  4. Fry the paste in the cracked cream until fragrant and the oil separates again.
  5. Simmer. Add the drumsticks and coat them in the paste. Add coconut milk and water. Cook ~30 minutes on low until the chicken is tender.
  6. Season. Fish sauce, palm sugar, dark soy (if using) — adjust to taste.
  7. Fry some noodles. Loosen a portion and deep-fry in hot oil until golden and crisp. Blanch the rest — barely, just to soften — and toss with a little oil so they don't clump.
  8. Bowl it. Noodles in, chicken and broth over, fried noodles on top. Condiments stay on the side — each eater seasons to taste.

Where Thai Sources Disagree

We don't hide the disagreements — we document them. These are the genuine variations between the Thai sources this recipe is built from:

Turmeric: fresh root (R-HAAN) vs powder (some home cooks) — either works.

Ginger vs galangal: R-HAAN uses ginger in the paste; some northern cooks use galangal. Both exist in Chiang Mai.

Cardamom: green pods (SGE), black cardamom (Kapook), or none (many home cooks).

Dark soy: SGE adds it for colour; R-HAAN seasons with fish sauce and palm sugar only.

Muslim/beef version: swaps curry powder for masala and often adds milk — the khao soi Islam tradition.

How Western Versions Get It Wrong

  • "Red curry paste" base. The most common shorthand — TasteAtlas itself describes khao soi this way. Khao soi has its own paste. Thai sources are explicit: substituting red curry paste gives a thinner, yellower, less aromatic broth.
  • Missing the fried-noodle crown. The dual noodle texture is the dish, not a garnish.
  • No pickled mustard greens. A defining condiment in every Thai source.
  • No turmeric. Colour and flavour both die without it.
  • "Light" coconut milk instead of cracked cream. The cracked cream is where the flavour base is built.

UK Ingredient Notes

  • Dried spur chillies (prik chee fa): Asian supermarkets. Dried Kashmiri or guajillo as a milder fallback.
  • Fresh turmeric, makrut limes, Thai shrimp paste: Asian grocers. Turmeric powder and frozen makrut zest work.
  • Palm sugar → light muscovado as fallback. Thai garlic → regular garlic.
  • Noodles: fresh flat egg noodles (the soft, loose kind), not dried spaghetti-style.
  • Half-batch: halve everything except the method — fine for 4 servings.
