Kaeng Ho (แกงโฮะ)

Northern Thai · Curries · Also called Khua Ho (คั่วโฮะ)

What is Kaeng Ho?

Kaeng Ho is the northern Thai dish that started as a way of never wasting temple food. Ho (โฮะ) means "to combine" — the dish is everything left over from a Buddhist festival stir-fried together with glass noodles, bamboo shoots and herbs. Its heart is almost always gaeng hang lay, the rich Burmese-influenced pork curry, which gives kaeng ho its deep spiced flavour.

Thai Wikipedia records the origin: after major merit-making festivals, villagers left large amounts of food at the temple. With no refrigerators, the monks' kitchen staff took leftover curries and stir-fries, rinsed them, and re-cooked everything together in one wok. The krua.co food-history team adds a detail lost from most accounts — in some villages, leftover curries were packed into clay jars buried in the ground with a thick seal of leftover sticky rice, and dug up weeks later as the base for a fresh batch. Today's kaeng ho is cooked from fresh ingredients, but it still reads as a deliberate mix: at least one curry base, pork, vegetables and noodles.

Ingredients

  • 200 g glass noodles (woon sen), soaked in cold water
  • 200 g pork shoulder, sliced thinly
  • 150 g gaeng hang lay, homemade or leftover
  • 1 tbsp red curry paste
  • 1 tbsp hang lay curry paste
  • 100 g pickled bamboo shoots, boiled and squeezed dry
  • 50 g pork crackling (kaeb moo)
  • 100 g pea aubergines (makheua phuang)
  • 1 Thai aubergine (makheua pro), cut into bites
  • 100 g yardlong beans, cut into 3 cm lengths
  • 1 cup ivy gourd leaves (tam lueng)
  • 5 kaffir lime leaves, torn
  • 2 stalks lemongrass, finely sliced
  • 5 cloves garlic, pounded
  • 2 tbsp fresh bird's eye chillies, pounded
  • 1 tbsp fish sauce
  • 1 tbsp lime juice
  • 3 tbsp vegetable oil

Method

  1. Soak the glass noodles in cold water until soft, then drain. Boil the pickled bamboo shoots in fresh water and squeeze well to remove the sourness — good bamboo should be tender and crunchy, not sharp.
  2. Heat the oil in a wok. Fry the pounded garlic until golden, then add the red curry paste and hang lay paste. Stir-fry until the paste smells rich and the oil starts to separate.
  3. Add the pork and stir-fry until cooked. Add the gaeng hang lay, bamboo shoots, pea aubergines, Thai aubergine, yardlong beans, lemongrass and half the kaffir lime leaves. Stir to combine.
  4. Season with fish sauce and lime juice. Add the drained glass noodles and stir-fry until they turn glossy and translucent — noodles go in last so they do not turn mushy.
  5. Fold in the ivy gourd leaves, pork crackling and remaining lime leaves. Serve hot with sticky rice.

Where Thai Sources Disagree

  • The curry base. Thai Wikipedia and krua.co hold to one base: gaeng hang lay. The nlovecooking recipe doubles it — hang lay paste plus red curry paste. Wongnai's Chothip goes furthest, stirring three curries (hang lay, red and green) plus curry powder into one wok. The single-base version is the classic; the rest are modern "richer" takes.
  • Naem and pork crackling. Only nlovecooking includes crumbled naem (fermented pork) alongside the crackling. Most northern recipes skip the naem — the sour-savoury job is done by the bamboo shoots.
  • Sourness. nlovecooking seasons with fish sauce and lime juice. Thai Wikipedia and Wongnai season with fish sauce (and soy sauce) only, letting the pickled bamboo carry the sour note.
  • History. Thai Wikipedia notes kaeng ho may descend from the Burmese hang lay itself and was born at temples. krua.co's account adds the buried-clay-jar storage tradition — a practice for keeping leftover curries edible for days before refrigeration.

Notes & Sources

Kaeng ho is a leftovers dish at heart — quantities are forgiving. The one rule every source agrees on: glass noodles go in last, or they melt into the curry. Vegetarian versions swap the pork for mushrooms and the fish sauce for light soy.
