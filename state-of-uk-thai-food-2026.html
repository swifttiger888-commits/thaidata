1. How Big Is the UK Thai Food Market?

As of June 2026, there are 1268 Thai food businesses registered with the Food Standards Agency (FSA) and Food Hygiene Information Scheme (FHIS for Scotland). This covers dine-in restaurants and takeaways — any establishment classified under Thai cuisine by their local authority.

📊 Key figure: 1,268 businesses across 254 UK cities. London alone accounts for 278 (21.9%). The average city has 2.1 Thai businesses per 100,000 residents (per-city average; the national per-capita figure is 2.0). The national average hygiene rating is ★4.4 out of 5.

The split between dine-in and takeaway varies significantly by city. Nationally, approximately 80% are dine-in restaurants and 20% are takeaways, though London skews more toward takeaway (17.3%) compared to cities like Sheffield (100% dine-in).

3. Where Are the Underserved Markets?

Per-capita density is the metric that matters for operators looking to expand. The UK average is 1.9 Thai businesses per 100,000 residents. Cities above this line are more competitive; cities below it represent opportunity.

🔍 Biggest gaps (high population, low Thai density): Birmingham (0.7/100k — 1.17M people but only 8 Thai businesses), Coventry (0.8/100k), and Nottingham (0.6/100k) all have large populations with disproportionately few Thai options.

York leads the density rankings at 11.6/100k, followed by Richmond upon Thames (8.2) and North Norfolk (6.8). These are the most competitive markets — but also the ones with proven demand.

7. 2026 Outlook: Minimum Wage, Margins & Migration

National Living Wage: £12.71/hr

The April 2026 NLW increase to £12.71/hr (up from £12.21 in April 2025, and £11.44 in 2024) hits hospitality harder than most sectors. A typical Thai restaurant with 6 front-of-house staff and 4 kitchen staff on minimum wage will see annual staff costs rise by approximately £22,000-£28,000. For a restaurant operating on 8-12% net margins, this is the difference between profit and loss.

Chef Shortages & Visa Rules

The Thai chef visa route remains one of the few viable paths for bringing skilled kitchen staff from Thailand. The Skilled Worker visa requires a minimum salary of £38,700 (or going rate, whichever is higher), sponsorship licence, and English language proficiency. In practice, this means only established restaurants can afford to sponsor.

Market Growth

Despite cost pressures, Thai cuisine remains one of the UK's most established Asian cuisines. The category's expansion is usually attributed to:
— Consumer demand for Southeast Asian flavours
— Relatively low barrier to entry vs fine dining
— Strong delivery platform performance for Thai food
— Growing "Thai street food" casual dining segment

8. Methodology & Data Sources

Primary source: Food Standards Agency (FSA) FHRS API — all establishments registered with UK local authorities. Filtered for Thai cuisine using business name and type matching.

Scotland: Food Hygiene Information Scheme (FHIS) — separate database for Scottish establishments.

Chain identification: Manual verification of major chains (Giggling Squid, Busaba, Rosa's Thai, Thaikhun, Chaophraya, etc.) to avoid double-counting.

Population data: ONS mid-2024 population estimates. Per-capita calculations use city/metropolitan populations, not wider urban areas.

Hygiene ratings: FSA 0-5 scale. "Pass" under FHIS (Scotland) mapped to 3-star for comparison purposes (Improvement Required → 1).

Menu prices: Manual collection from restaurant websites and delivery platforms (Just Eat, Deliveroo, Uber Eats), 75 data points across 5 cities. Updated quarterly.

Limitations: 122 premises were excluded due to lack of inspection data. Some businesses may operate under broader "Asian" or "Oriental" categories and not appear in Thai-filtered results. The actual number of businesses serving Thai food in the UK is likely higher than 1268.
