What is Songkran?

Songkran (สงกรานต์) is the Thai New Year — the most important festival in Thai culture. Traditionally celebrated April 13–15, it marks the sun's transition into Aries and the end of the dry season. It's famous worldwide for its water fights, but in the UK, celebrations are more traditional: water pouring ceremonies over Buddha images and elders' hands (rod nam dum hua), merit-making at temples, sand pagoda building, and massive Thai food gatherings.

In the UK, Songkran is the single biggest annual gathering of the Thai community — over 5,000 people attend Wat Buddhapadipa in Wimbledon alone. For restaurants, it's the peak marketing opportunity of the year.

Where to Celebrate in 2026

🏴󠁧󠁢󠁥󠁮󠁧󠁿 London
Wat Buddhapadipa
UK's Largest Songkran
The flagship event. Traditional alms-giving to monks at dawn, water blessing ceremony, sand pagoda building (a uniquely Thai New Year tradition), Thai food stalls serving everything from som tam to mango sticky rice, cultural dance performances, and craft stalls. 5,000+ people. Parking is limited — arrive early or use Wimbledon station (15-min walk). Free entry, donations welcome.
🏴󠁧󠁢󠁥󠁮󠁧󠁿 Runcorn
Wat Phra Singh UK
Northern England's Songkran Hub
The North's largest Songkran. Water pouring, traditional Lanna-style music and dance, community-prepared Thai food. Building a new Ubosot Hall — see the construction progress. Easy access from Manchester, Liverpool, and Leeds. Free entry with ample parking.
🏴󠁧󠁢󠁥󠁮󠁧󠁿 Manchester
Wat Charoenbhavana
Salford Community Songkran
Manchester's Thai community gathers for merit-making, monk offerings, and water blessing. Indoor and outdoor events. The converted factory setting gives it a uniquely Mancunian character. Free entry.
🏴󠁧󠁢󠁥󠁮󠁧󠁿 Birmingham
Wat Mahathat UK
Midlands Countryside Songkran
Peaceful countryside temple near Lichfield. Led by Ajahn Laow, one of the UK's most respected Thai abbots. Traditional ceremonies, Thai street food, cultural shows. Less crowded than the London event — a more intimate experience. Free entry.
🏴󠁧󠁢󠁷󠁬󠁳󠁿 Swansea
Wat Sanghapadipa
Wales' Only Thai Temple Songkran
The Welsh Thai community's biggest annual event. Water ceremonies, Thai food, and cultural activities in Wales' only Thai Buddhist temple. A unique celebration — smaller scale but warm community atmosphere. Free entry.
🏴󠁧󠁢󠁳󠁣󠁴󠁿 Scotland
Wat Phra Dhammakaya
Scottish Songkran
Scotland's Thai Buddhist centre in Helensburgh. Water ceremonies, meditation, and community food. Smaller than the English events but the only dedicated Songkran in Scotland. Check Facebook for 2026 dates.

Songkran Traditions You'll See

Rod Nam Dum Hua (รดน้ำดำหัว) — The water pouring ceremony. Younger people pour scented water over the hands of elders and monks to ask for blessings. This is the spiritual heart of Songkran — not the water fights you see in Thailand tourism videos.

Sand Pagoda Building (ก่อเจดีย์ทราย) — Families build intricate sand pagodas at temple grounds, decorated with flowers and flags. It's believed to return the sand carried away on your shoes during the year. Wat Buddhapadipa's sand pagodas are particularly elaborate.

Making Merit (ทำบุญ) — Offering food to monks at dawn, releasing birds or fish, and donating to the temple. Most UK temples hold an early morning alms round — arrive by 7am if you want to participate.

Song Nam Phra (สรงน้ำพระ) — Pouring scented water over Buddha images. A purification ritual. Every UK temple sets up a Buddha statue for this purpose during Songkran.

Songkran at Restaurants

Major Thai restaurant chains run Songkran promotions throughout April. These tend to be more commercial but are more accessible if you can't make it to a temple:

  • Rosa's Thai Cafe — 20+ UK locations. Typically runs a limited-edition Songkran menu with seasonal specials. Check their website from late March.
  • Chaophraya — Flagship restaurants in major cities. Hosts ticketed Songkran dinner events with set menus and cultural performances.
  • Thaikhun — Street-food style chain. Songkran specials and themed decor throughout April.
  • Giggling Squid — 50+ UK locations. Usually runs a Songkran set menu.
  • Independent Thai restaurants — Many run their own Songkran events. Check your local Thai restaurant's social media in late March.
