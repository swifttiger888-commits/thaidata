Key Findings

  • Giggling Squid and Rosa's Thai dominate — between them they account for 79 of the 131+ chain locations (60%)
  • London-centric — Busaba (7/8) and Patara (5/5) are almost exclusively in London, while Giggling Squid has the widest geographic footprint
  • Hygiene scores are high — 89% of chain locations hold a 5-star FSA rating, well above the national average of ~50% across all UK Thai restaurants
  • Chain consolidation — Thai Khun (10 locations, 6 English cities) is owned by Giggling Squid, giving the parent group 57+ locations total
  • FSS data gap — Scotland uses Food Standards Scotland (Pass/Fail), not the 0–5 rating system. FSA hygiene scores for 3 of Chaophraya's 5 locations and 2 of Thai Khun's 10 locations are not available

Chain Profiles

Giggling Squid

47 locations across 44 UK towns and cities
100% 5-star FSA hygiene rating
National — widest geographic footprint of any UK Thai chain

The largest Thai restaurant chain in the UK by a significant margin. Founded in 2002 as a single restaurant in Brighton, Giggling Squid now operates from Exeter to Edinburgh, with particular density in Southern England. Every single one of its 47 FSA-listed premises holds the maximum 5-star hygiene rating — the cleanest record of any chain in this comparison. The brand also owns Thai Khun, adding 10 more locations under a different trading name.

Rosa's Thai

32 locations across England
97% 5-star FSA hygiene rating
London-heavy — 18 of 32 in the capital

The second-largest Thai chain, founded in 2008 in Spitalfields. Rosa's Thai has expanded aggressively into outer London boroughs and regional cities including Bristol, Reading, Norwich and Exeter. 31 of its 32 FSA-rated locations hold 5 stars; the remaining Scottish premises is FSS-regulated (Pass, not numerically rated). The brand positions itself as casual, affordable Thai with strong breakfast and brunch offerings alongside standard dinner service.

Busaba

8 locations
62% 5-star FSA hygiene rating
London-focused — 7 of 8 in the capital

One of the best-known Thai restaurant brands in the UK, founded by Alan Yau (also behind Wagamama and Hakkasan). Busaba entered administration in July 2025 but continues to trade while a buyer is sought. Despite only 8 locations, Busaba carries disproportionate brand weight in the London dining scene — its dark-wood, communal-table aesthetic defined a generation of Thai casual dining. All 8 locations hold FSA ratings between 3 and 5, with 5 of 8 at the maximum 5 stars.

Chaophraya

5 locations
5.0/5 avg (FSA-rated English locations only)
National — Newcastle, Birmingham, Glasgow, Edinburgh, Aberdeen

The premium end of UK Thai chains. Chaophraya's restaurants are destination venues — multi-storey spaces in landmark buildings with rooftop terraces and cocktail bars. Both English locations (Newcastle and Birmingham) hold 5-star FSA ratings. The three Scottish locations (Glasgow, Edinburgh, Aberdeen) are regulated by Food Standards Scotland and have received a 'Pass' — the highest available rating under the Scottish system. Chaophraya's average customer spend is notably higher than the other chains in this comparison.

Thai Express

12 locations
4.1/5 avg FSA hygiene rating
Regional — concentrated in London and Midlands

The third-largest Thai chain by location count. More variable in its offer than the top two — a mix of full-service restaurants and quick-service food-court outlets. 7 of 12 locations hold 5-star ratings, but one premises currently carries an 'Improvement Required' notice from FSA inspection. The chain's presence is strongest in London (3), with Midlands locations in Birmingham and Swansea and a single London-fringe outlet in Thurrock.

Thai Khun

10 locations
4.8/5 avg FSA hygiene rating (English locations)
Regional — 6 cities, Scottish presence

Owned by Giggling Squid, Thai Khun serves as the group's secondary brand — positioned as a more casual, street-food-inspired concept. 8 of 10 locations have FSA ratings (averaging 4.8/5 with 6 at 5 stars); the remaining 2 in Scotland are FSS-regulated (Pass). The brand has particular density in university cities (Cambridge, Southampton, Bath, Oxford), suggesting a strategic focus on the student and young-professional market.

Methodology & Data Sources

Data sourced from the Food Standards Agency (FSA) Food Hygiene Ratings API, June 2026. Scottish locations regulated by Food Standards Scotland (FSS) are noted separately — FSS uses a Pass/Fail system and does not assign the 0–5 numerical rating used by the FSA in England, Wales, and Northern Ireland. Location counts reflect active registered premises at the time of data collection. Chain identification is based on business name matching against known Thai restaurant chain brands. Chains with fewer than 5 locations or ambiguous name matching are excluded from this comparison.
